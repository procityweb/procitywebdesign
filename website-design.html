Examples of pages you could have

Every Page on Your Website Has a Job

These are examples of pages your website could have, or might need. You won't need every one, and your list may look different from another business's. Pick the pages that match what you offer and how your customers search, and I’ll help you decide. Here's what each one does for you.

Home

Your front door, and usually the first page people see. It shows who you are and what you do in a few seconds, builds trust with your best photos and reviews, and points visitors to the next step, like calling or asking for a quote.

About Us

Your story. People hire people they trust, and this is where they get to know you. Share who's behind the business, how long you've been at it and what you care about. Just opened? Tell them why you started. Been around a while? Show off your track record.

Services

A page for each service you offer, so a customer can find exactly what they need. It also lets Google match each service to the specific searches people make, like “water heater repair” or “wedding photographer.”

Service Areas

Pages for the towns and cities you serve. When someone searches “plumber in Phoenix,” a page about your Phoenix work gives Google a reason to show you, instead of a business that only mentions one city.

Photo Gallery

Your best work on display. Finished jobs, before-and-afters, your team, your space. Photos sell faster than words, and they let people picture working with you before they ever pick up the phone.

Reviews

Happy customers doing the selling for you. Real feedback builds trust fast, especially with people who've never heard of you. Just opened? We add this page as soon as you have your first few reviews.

FAQ

Answers the questions customers always ask, like pricing, hours and how it works, so they don't have to call to find out. It also helps with Google: when someone searches a local question, Google often pulls the answer from a business's FAQ page if it answers that question directly. That can put your name in front of people who haven't chosen anyone yet.

Contact and Quotes

Makes it effortless to reach you: tap to call, tap to text, or send a short quote request. Your hours, address and directions live here too. People who aren't ready to call will often send a form instead, so you don't lose them.

Don't see a page you need? We can build it. Other pages our clients ask for include a Menu, Pricing, Our Team, Financing, Emergency Service and Special Offers.

So how does Google decide which of those pages to show? It works like a library↓
Why more pages win more customers

Think of Google as the World's Biggest Library

So why do pages matter so much? Every page on your website is like a book on a shelf with your business name on the cover. When someone searches on Google, Google acts like a librarian. It walks the shelves and hands that person the book that best matches what they asked for.

So when a customer types:

brake repair near me

Let's say it's 7 a.m., the car starts grinding every time the driver hits the brakes, and they grab their phone and type that search. They're stressed, they're ready to pay, and they'll call the first company that looks like it can help.

With a one-page website, Google looks at your single page, sees it's mostly about your business in general, and decides another company's page is a better match. That driver ends up calling a different company.

Now picture your website with a page just about brake repair. Google finds the perfect match on your shelf and puts your business right in front of that driver. They tap to call, and the job is yours.

Now multiply that by every service you offer. Oil changes. Check engine lights. New tires. Every one of those is a different search, by a different customer, every single day. Each page you add is another door those customers can walk through.

If your salon has a one-page website that says "haircuts, color and more," Google doesn't see a match for bridal styling. So it shows her the salon down the street that has a page called Bridal Hair and Makeup. She books them, and brings her whole bridal party with her.

If your website has that Bridal page, Google hands her your book. One page. One search. A whole wedding party of new clients who may come back for years.

Now think about balayage, extensions, kids' cuts and keratin treatments. Each one is a different person, searching for a different service, ready to book today.

Website Design Questions

How does the free preview work?

Call or fill out the form, and I’ll call you within 30 minutes. Tell me about your business, and I build your homepage first. You see it, tell me what you'd change, and only then decide. If you love it, you pay the setup price and I build the rest. If you don't, you owe nothing.

I’m just starting my business, or I don't have a website. Is that okay?

Absolutely. Many of our clients are opening their doors for the first time or have never had a website. We build your whole website from scratch, including the writing and photos, and guide you through every step in plain English. You don't need reviews, a long history or any website know-how to get started.

How many pages do I need?

Most local businesses do best with 5 pages to start: Home, About, Services, Photo Gallery and Contact. If you offer several services or serve several towns, 10 pages lets each one have its own page, which helps you show up for more searches.

Do I need to write anything?

No. Professional writing and photos are free with every website. I just need a quick call to learn about your business, and I take it from there.

Can I see my website before it goes live?

Yes. On day 6 you get to look it over and tell me what you'd like changed before it launches.

Can I add more pages later?

Absolutely. Your website can grow as your business grows. Just give me a call.

What does the $59 a month cover, and is there a contract?

Your website is a one-time setup price, $99 for 5 pages or $199 for 10 pages. The $59-a-month Website Care Plan keeps it online: hosting, fast loading, the security lock (SSL), updates and small edits, and help with your web address. There is no contract, so you can cancel anytime. Our marketing services, like SEO, Google Ads and Facebook and Instagram ads, are separate, optional, and $199 a month each.

Who owns my domain name?

You do. You buy your own web address (called a domain) from a registrar, usually for about $10 to $20 a year, so it always belongs to you. I help you pick one and connect it to your new website. Already have a domain? I’ll connect that one.

What isn't included in the price?

To keep pricing simple and affordable, a few things are not included, but I’m happy to talk about custom pricing for them: complex custom web applications, large online stores (100+ products), advanced custom integrations, custom photo and video shoots, ongoing social media management, ongoing content writing, and SEO and ads (those are $199 a month each). Your domain name is bought by you, and ad budgets are paid directly to Google or Facebook.

What happens if I cancel?

You can cancel anytime, with no contract and no cancellation fee. Your Care Plan ends at the end of the month you've paid for. You keep your domain, your logo and your social media pages, and on request I’ll send you your website's files so you can host them elsewhere.
