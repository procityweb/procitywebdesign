Why five pages often make sense

Think of each page as having one job. With five pages, your main services each get a page of their own, so a customer searching for exactly that lands on a page made for them, and Google has five pages to choose from instead of one.

  • People search for specific things. Someone looking for deep cleaning is more likely to call the company with a page about deep cleaning.
  • Visitors find answers faster, because each main service has its own page and nobody has to scroll through everything.
  • Google can match each page to the search it answers best, so you can show up for more than one kind of search.

An honest note about Google

Do You Want to Be Found for More Than One Service?

If you do, it helps to know one thing most people never hear: Google ranks pages, not websites.

We tend to picture Google ranking whole companies, like a list. It doesn't work that way. When someone searches for “deep cleaning,” Google doesn't hunt for the best cleaning company. It looks for the single best page about deep cleaning, and shows that one.

Every page on your website has its own web address, and Google judges each address on its own. A page that talks about everything is rarely the best answer to anything. A page about one thing, written for that one search, has a real chance.

  • Every page has its own addressLike yourcleaning.com/deep-cleaning
  • Google picks the best page for each searchNot the best website overall
  • More pages, more searches you can winWhen each page has something real to say

So the honest question is: do you want to be found for a few main services, or for a wide variety of services and areas? If it's a few, 5 pages does the job well. If it's many, 10 pages gives each one its own page. More pages only help when you have something real to say on each, so if fewer will do, I’ll tell you.
